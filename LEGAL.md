# ClipCast Studio — Legal Responsibilities

**Read this before posting your first video.**

This document is a plain-English summary of your legal responsibilities when
using ClipCast Studio. It is not legal advice. If you have specific concerns,
consult a qualified attorney.

---

## 1. Twitch Clip Ownership

Twitch clips are created from streams broadcast on Twitch's platform.

- **The streamer owns their performance and likeness.** Clips are derivative
  works of the streamer's content.
- **Twitch's Terms of Service** grant viewers the ability to create clips, but
  that permission is limited to personal, non-commercial use unless the streamer
  has explicitly granted broader rights.
- **Reposting clips to TikTok for monetization** falls into a gray area. Many
  streamers actively support clip channels. Many do not. You are responsible for
  knowing the preferences of the streamers whose content you use.
- **Best practice:** Get explicit written permission from streamers before
  monetizing their clips. Always credit them visibly in your video and caption.
  ClipCast Studio always appends a credit line to every caption for this reason.

---

## 2. YouTube Terms of Service & Downloading

YouTube's Terms of Service (Section 5B) prohibit downloading videos without
YouTube's express written permission or unless a download button is provided.

- **yt-dlp downloads may violate YouTube's ToS.** Using it to download and
  repost YouTube content is done at your own risk.
- YouTube creators own the copyright to their content. Reposting their videos —
  even clipped — without permission is copyright infringement under the DMCA.
- **Fair use** (parody, commentary, criticism, education) may apply in some
  cases, but short gaming highlight clips generally do not qualify.
- **Best practice:** Only use content from creators who have explicitly given
  permission for clipping and reposting, or content licensed under Creative
  Commons. Always credit the original creator.

---

## 3. Music Copyright and DMCA Risk

Background music in gaming clips is the single largest source of DMCA takedowns
on TikTok, YouTube, and Instagram.

- Music playing in a clip — even if the streamer was playing it in the
  background — is still subject to the original rights holder's copyright.
- TikTok, YouTube, and Instagram all have automated Content ID / Rights Manager
  systems that detect copyrighted music and may mute, restrict, or remove your
  video, or monetize it on behalf of the rights holder.
- ClipCast Studio includes a **music detector** that flags clips that likely
  contain background music. Heed these warnings seriously.
- **Best practice:** Before posting any clip with music, verify the music is
  either rights-cleared, royalty-free, or covered by a streaming license. When
  in doubt, don't post it.

---

## 4. Streamer Attribution — Always Required

ClipCast Studio **always** appends a credit line (e.g., `clip via xQc`) to
every generated TikTok/YouTube/Instagram caption. This is non-negotiable and
cannot be disabled.

Attribution is both:
- **Ethically important** — streamers work hard to create the content you are
  building on.
- **Legally relevant** — some copyright fair-use defenses require attribution,
  and many platform content policies require it for derivative content.

---

## 5. Monetization

Earning money from clips of other people's content without permission is a
significant legal risk.

- Ad revenue, TikTok Creator Rewards, brand deals, and Super Chat on content
  you do not own may expose you to legal action.
- Some streamers actively support clip channels and may grant permission
  informally or formally. Get it in writing.
- The `permissions` column in ClipCast Studio's database is reserved for
  tracking which streamers have given explicit consent. Use it.
